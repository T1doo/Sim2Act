Two P2 contract clarifications were sent before any implementation:

1. The proposal says complete T0/T1 projections must be equal but also allows heartbeat growth constrained by min(current lease,L0). Choose explicitly: either any Run lease change causes safe refusal (including heartbeat growth), or only that dynamic field can grow under unchanged owner/version/fence/etc while the effective cap stays L0. Do not silently exempt other Run/header/history/collection differences as heartbeat effects. Store.heartbeat (`db.py:1370`) currently updates lease_until; its state publication and writer lock are separate (`text_workflows.py:895/926`). General whole-row equality and permitted lease growth must not be conflated.
2. Enforce L0 and other applicable original finite bounds after acquiring T1's write lock, before new protected reads or any result/proof publication, then retain all later original points and the post-scope final clock. Busy/lock waiting must count. Checking only at the last write is insufficient because current Store.guard accepts a potentially later renewed lease. Compute past L0 cannot be revived by opening a fresh T1. The final cap remains min(current effective lease,L0,all original deadlines), not a replacement for the admission-time cap check.

Conditional correctness argument is acceptable only with those premises: immutable S; every actually consumed Q re-read under the same T1 and matched under a documented equality/dynamic-field rule; current authorization/ownership/finite constraints; no unaccounted callback or read-set change between comparison and writes; original postwrite expected delta verified before commit. It does not prove that all transient between-transaction changes are observable, or that every arbitrary callback remains pure. Relevant history/revision changes must be part of Q where original semantics consume them; final-state comparison is not an invented global ABA detector.

Official SQLite documentation independently checked: [Isolation](https://www.sqlite.org/isolation.html) describes one writer at a time, BEGIN IMMEDIATE blocking other writers, and visibility of a connection's own writes. [Transactions](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_transaction.html) states BEGIN IMMEDIATE acquires a write transaction and may itself fail busy. These facts support the proposed same-T1 exclusion argument, not cross-connection token reuse, absence of own-connection mutations, a performance guarantee, or PostgreSQL predicate protection.
